Why your business doesn't show up when customers ask ChatGPT

More and more buyers ask an AI assistant who to hire before they ever open Google. If it never names you, it's usually not personal — the model just never built a clear picture of your business. Here's why, and how to fix it.

The short version

It's usually not that AI is excluding you

When an AI assistant doesn't name your business, it's rarely a deliberate snub. These tools don't search the way Google does — they assemble an answer from what they've already read across the open web, then name a small handful of businesses they can read cleanly and trust. If your business is hard to read, inconsistent across the web, or missing from the sources they cross-reference, you're left out of the answer — even when you're the better choice. Every one of those is fixable.

Why you're missing

The usual reasons — and the pillar each one sits under

These are the causes AI assistants themselves point to when you ask them. Each maps to one of the six things we check in the Eko Standard.

Reachable

AI crawlers can't get in

If bots like OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot or PerplexityBot are blocked in your robots.txt, or by your host or CDN, the assistant can't read your site at all — so it can't name you. It's the most complete way to be invisible, and one of the easiest to overlook.

Structured

Your answers are buried

AI models don't read a page like a person. They scan for a concise block of text that directly answers the question. If what you do and who you serve is wrapped in a slogan or sits below the fold, the model skips past it. Stating it plainly in the first few lines of a page changes what the model can quote. More on what content gets cited by AI.

Declared

Your details don't line up

Assistants cross-reference your name, address, phone number and services across your site, your listings and your profiles. When those disagree, the model hedges — or picks a competitor whose details are consistent. Clean, matching facts everywhere (and structured data behind them) let it name you with confidence. Your Google Business Profile is part of this too.

Citable

Nothing corroborates you

These tools work on a kind of consensus: they trust a business more when independent sources — directories, industry sites, forums, reviews — say the same thing. If you exist only on your own website, there's nothing to cross-check, and the model stays cautious. Thin or missing reviews weaken the same signal — here's how many reviews actually move the needle.

Convertible

Even when named, the trail goes cold

Being mentioned is only half of it. If the page a buyer lands on doesn't make the next step obvious — contact, quote, book — the mention doesn't turn into work. Readable to AI and clear to a human are the same discipline done well.

Maintained

What worked last quarter has drifted

Models change, competitors move, and answers get re-generated fresh each time. A business that was named in June can quietly drop out by spring. Visibility isn't a one-off fix — it's checked and held, which is why it needs re-testing, not just building once.

Questions people ask

How do you get your business listed on ChatGPT?

There is no directory to submit to. ChatGPT does not keep a master list of businesses and cannot guarantee inclusion — it assembles each answer from what it can read and trust across the open web at the time it is asked. So you do not get listed; you become readable, consistent and corroborated enough that the model names you. That is the work.

How do I get my business to show up in AI searches?

Make sure AI crawlers can reach your site, state plainly what you do and who you serve in the first few lines of each page, keep your name, address and phone number identical everywhere they appear, earn mentions on independent sources the models already trust, and keep genuine reviews current. No one can guarantee a mention, but those are the signals AI assistants actually draw on.

Why does AI recommend my competitor and not me?

Usually it is not that the competitor is better — it is that they are easier for the model to read, more consistent across the web, and corroborated by more sources it trusts. AI names only a handful of businesses per answer and picks the ones it can parse and cross-reference with confidence. That gap is fixable.

Do I need to let AI crawlers onto my site?

Yes. If bots like OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot or PerplexityBot are blocked in your robots.txt or by your host or CDN, those assistants cannot read your site at all — so they cannot name you. Checking and unblocking crawler access is one of the first things to fix, and it is easy to overlook.
