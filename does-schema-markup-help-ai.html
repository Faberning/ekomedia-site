Does schema markup help AI recommend your business?

You may have been offered “AI schema” as the way to get named by ChatGPT or Google’s AI. Schema markup is real and useful, but it’s often oversold. Here’s what it does, what Google and Microsoft say about it, and where it sits on a New Zealand business’s to-do list.

The short answer

It helps, but it isn’t a switch. Schema markup is code that labels the facts on your page, such as your business name, address, services and opening hours, so machines can read them without guessing. Microsoft says schema helps its search and AI systems understand content. Google says there’s no special schema you need to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, but any structured data you have must match the visible text on the page. So add it once your page and your Google Business Profile tell the same story. It can’t get a business named whose facts don’t add up.

What is schema markup?

Schema markup (also called structured data) is a short block of code, usually JSON-LD, that sits in your page and describes it using a shared vocabulary from schema.org. People don’t see it. Search engines and AI systems read it as a set of labelled facts: this is a plumber, this is the phone number, these are the areas it serves.

Here’s what basic business markup looks like. It’s a made-up example, not a real business:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Plumber",
  "name": "Example Plumbing Ltd",
  "url": "https://exampleplumbing.co.nz/",
  "telephone": "+64 9 000 0000",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "1 Example Street",
    "addressLocality": "Orewa",
    "postalCode": "0931",
    "addressCountry": "NZ"
  },
  "areaServed": [
    "Orewa",
    "Silverdale",
    "Whangaparāoa"
  ],
  "openingHours": "Mo-Fr 07:00-17:00",
  "sameAs": [
    "https://www.facebook.com/exampleplumbing"
  ]
}

Example only. Every value in your own markup must match what your page and your Google Business Profile say.

What do Google and Microsoft actually say?

  • Google: no special schema needed for AI features. Google’s guide “AI features and your website” says “There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode” and “There’s also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add.” It still lists “Making sure your structured data matches the visible text on the page” and keeping your Business Profile up to date as worthwhile.
  • Microsoft: schema helps its AI understand you. Microsoft’s October 2025 guide to appearing in AI search answers says “Schema is a type of code that helps search engines and AI systems understand your content.” Bing’s index powers Microsoft Copilot.

Put together: schema is a clear way to state your facts, and at least one AI search provider says it uses it. Neither says markup on its own decides who gets recommended.

Which schema matters for a local business?

  • Your business details. LocalBusiness (or a more specific type such as Plumber or Dentist) or Organization: name, address, phone, opening hours, the areas you serve, and links to your Google Business Profile and social pages.
  • Your services. One Service entry per main service, on the page that describes it.
  • Your FAQs, if they’re on the page. FAQPage markup only for questions and answers people can actually see. Hidden or made-up Q&A breaks the “matches the visible text” rule.
  • Breadcrumbs. BreadcrumbList tells machines how your pages fit together.
  • Not your own review stars. Google doesn’t show review stars that a business marks up about itself on its own site. Real reviews on your Google profile do far more.

What can’t schema do?

It can’t fix a site that blocks AI crawlers (see which bots matter), a Google Business Profile that disagrees with your website, or a business that has few reviews and no mentions anywhere else. AI assistants pick businesses whose facts agree across many sources. Schema states your side of it clearly. It doesn’t create the agreement.

We haven’t seen a dated, logged-out test showing that markup alone made an AI name a business. If a provider claims that, ask to see one.

How do I check my own schema?

  • Run Google’s Rich Results Test. Paste your page address into search.google.com/test/rich-results to see what Google can read and any errors.
  • Run the Schema Markup Validator. validator.schema.org shows every type on the page, including ones Google doesn’t use for rich results.
  • Compare it with your page and profile. Check that the name, address, phone, hours and areas in the markup match the page text and your Google Business Profile, word for word.

Related questions
Yes. Google and Bing both still read structured data, and Microsoft says it helps its AI systems understand your content. What’s changed is the hype: Google says there’s no special schema needed for AI Overviews or AI Mode, so treat it as part of a well-built page, not a separate AI trick.
It isn’t a direct ranking boost. It helps search engines understand a page and can make it eligible for rich results, such as breadcrumbs or event details in Google. The bigger value for a local business is that your name, address, phone, services and areas are stated clearly and match everywhere else they appear.
Add it as a JSON-LD script in the page’s code, usually through your website builder, a plugin or your developer. Start with your business details (LocalBusiness or Organization), then your services, and an FAQ block only for questions that are visible on the page. Test it with Google’s Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator.
Be careful. Schema is a small, one-off technical job, and no type of markup makes ChatGPT or Google name you on its own. If someone sells it as an AI fix, ask them to show a dated, logged-out test where an AI named a business because of the markup, and what else they changed.
