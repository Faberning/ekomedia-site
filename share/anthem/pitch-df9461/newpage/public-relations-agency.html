Public relations agency or in-house team?

When an agency makes sense, when an in-house team does, and how the two work together.

Quick answer

A public relations agency helps an organisation earn attention and trust: media relations, crisis and issues work, government relations and corporate announcements. Agencies suit senior advice, specialist skills and extra capacity; in-house teams suit steady day-to-day work. Many organisations use both. Anthem is a PR-led agency in the Auckland CBD.

Question 1

What does a public relations agency do?

It helps you earn attention and trust from media, customers, staff, investors and Government, and protects your reputation when things go wrong.

Question 2

Should we hire a PR agency or build an in-house team?

An agency suits senior advice, specialist skills such as public affairs, campaign capacity and crisis support. In-house suits steady daily work that needs deep knowledge of the business. Many organisations have both.

Question 3

How do PR agency retainers work?

You agree a monthly scope (advice, media relations, content, reporting) with a named team, reviewed regularly. Project fees suit one-off campaigns.

Question 4

What should be in a PR brief?

What you want to change, who you need to reach, what success looks like, your timeline and budget range, and anything sensitive the agency needs to know.

Question 5

How do you measure PR results?

Against goals set at the start: coverage in the outlets your audience reads, stakeholder outcomes, website visits and enquiries, and whether AI tools name you correctly.

Question 6

Can a PR agency work alongside our in-house team?

Yes. The in-house team runs the day to day; the agency brings strategy, specialists and surge support. Anthem works this way with many clients.
