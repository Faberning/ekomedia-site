How does a house valuation work in Auckland?

There are two kinds of "valuation" people mean. A market appraisal is an agent’s view of what your home would likely sell for now, based on recent comparable sales. It is free. A registered valuation is a formal report from a registered valuer, usually needed when a bank, a lawyer or the family court asks for one, and you pay for it.

Most sellers start with an appraisal. If your situation needs a registered valuation, I will tell you so and point you to a local valuer.

03

Appraisal or registered valuation: which do you need?

If you are deciding whether to sell, or what price to aim for, an appraisal is usually enough. If you are refinancing, settling an estate, separating, or a buyer’s bank wants an independent figure, you will likely need a registered valuation.

The two can differ. A valuer works to a formal standard and can be conservative; an appraisal reads what buyers are paying on your street this month. Knowing both is useful when the stakes are high.

Online estimates vs a real appraisal

Property websites give an automated estimate from sales data and the council record. They are a useful starting point, but the software has never been inside your home. It does not know about the new kitchen, the extra room downstairs, the sea glimpse from the deck, or the deck that is not on the title.

Treat an online number as a rough range. An appraisal takes that range and adjusts it for the things only a visit shows.

We walk through the house and the section, look at anything you are unsure about (a leaky gutter, an old deck, a sleepout), and talk about who is likely to buy it. It helps to have your title, any council consents and a list of improvements handy.

08

Auction, deadline sale or asking price?

An auction creates a fixed date and competition, and suits homes that will draw several buyers. A deadline sale sets a date for written offers without a public price. An asking price is simple and suits homes with a clear market value.

There is no single right answer for Stanmore Bay. We choose together after the appraisal, based on your home and what is selling on the Peninsula right now.

Selling a cross-lease home

Many Stanmore Bay homes are on cross-lease titles. Buyers’ lawyers check that the flats plan matches the buildings on the section. A deck, carport or extension that is not on the plan can slow a sale or give a buyer a reason to renegotiate.

We check your title early. If something is missing, there is time to talk to a surveyor or a lawyer about fixing it before buyers ask.

Real estate commission in NZ: what selling costs

Selling costs usually include agent commission, marketing (photos, online listings, signage) and your lawyer’s fees. Commission is negotiable and is set out in the agency agreement.

Before you sign anything, you get the Real Estate Authority’s guide to agency agreements and a written estimate of the commission at the likely sale price, so you know the full cost up front. No surprises after the sale.
