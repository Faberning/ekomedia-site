What should I look for in an Auckland mortgage broker?

Look for a licensed adviser with a wide lender panel, clear disclosure of how they are paid, and experience with buyers like you.

  • They work for a licensed Financial Advice Provider and are on the Financial Service Providers Register.
  • They can show you options from more than one bank.
  • They explain fees and commission before giving advice.

How do I check an adviser is registered?

Search their name or company on the Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR). It is free and public.

Does a mortgage broker cost me anything?

Most advisers are paid a commission by the lender, so many clients pay no fee. Any fee, and when it would apply, must be disclosed before advice is given.

Do I need to live near the office?

What does a broker do that a bank does not?

A bank can only offer its own loans. A broker compares loans from a range of lenders and helps you choose the one that suits you.

What are the steps?

First chat, gathering your documents, choosing a loan structure, applying to lenders, meeting the approval conditions, then settlement.

  • First chat: your goals, income and deposit.
  • Documents: payslips, bank statements, ID.
  • Structure: how much fixed or floating, and for how long.
  • Application: sent to the lenders that fit.
  • Settlement: we work with your lawyer so the money is ready on the day.

How are mortgage brokers paid?

Usually by the lender, as a commission once the loan settles. This must be disclosed to you.

  • [MortgageDesign disclosure wording]

Will applying through a broker hurt my credit?

Lenders may run a credit check when you apply. A broker helps you apply to the right lenders rather than many at once.

What happens after settlement?

We stay in touch. When your fixed rate is due to end, we look at your options again.

Banks and other lenders, compared

Who lends to whom, and why it matters for your application.

Clayton D’Lima, mortgage adviser, MortgageDesign

What is the difference between a bank and a non-bank lender?

Banks lend to most borrowers. Non-bank lenders often help people whose situation does not fit a bank, such as new businesses or past credit issues.

When does a non-bank lender make sense?

When a bank says no, or would take too long, for reasons like self-employed income or a short trading history.

  • Rates and fees are often higher, so it is usually a step towards a bank loan later.

Can I move to a bank later?

Often, yes. Once your situation is clearer, refinancing to a bank may be possible.

  • Check any break fees or clawbacks first.

Which lenders does MortgageDesign work with?

We work with a panel of banks and non-bank lenders.

  • [lender panel from the Important Information page]

Is the lowest rate always the best loan?

Not always. Fees, cashback, flexibility and how the loan is structured all matter too.

Mortgage loans, explained simply

Terms, deposits and repayments in plain English.

Clayton D’Lima, mortgage adviser, MortgageDesign
On this page
  1. How does a mortgage loan work?
  2. What is the difference between table and interest-only loans?
  3. What is a revolving credit or offset loan?
  4. What fees come with a mortgage?
  5. How much can I borrow?

How does a mortgage loan work?

A lender pays for most of the home, and you repay that loan plus interest over an agreed term, often up to 30 years.

What is the difference between table and interest-only loans?

With a table loan, each repayment covers interest and some of the loan. With interest-only, you pay just the interest for a set time.

  • Interest-only is more common for investors and has a time limit.

What is a revolving credit or offset loan?

Loans that let your savings or income reduce the interest you pay, while keeping access to the money.

What fees come with a mortgage?

There may be application, valuation and legal costs, and break fees if you end a fixed rate early.

How much can I borrow?

It depends on your income, spending, debts and deposit. We work it out with you at the first meeting.

How lending works here, and what has changed recently.

Who sets the lending rules in New Zealand?

Lenders set their own policies, within limits set by the Reserve Bank and consumer lending law.

What are LVR and DTI limits?

LVR limits restrict how much banks lend to people with small deposits. DTI limits restrict lending that is large compared with income.

  • [current Reserve Bank settings, checked on the day]

Are new builds treated differently?

Yes. New builds are exempt from the LVR limits, so some lenders accept smaller deposits for them.

Why do lenders check my spending?

Consumer lending law requires lenders to make sure a loan is affordable, which includes looking at your regular spending.

Can someone from overseas get a home loan here?

It depends on residency and visa status. Overseas buyer rules also limit who can buy.

Fixed, floating or split?

How each one works, and the questions to ask before you choose.

Clayton D’Lima, mortgage adviser, MortgageDesign
On this page
  1. What is a fixed rate?
  2. What is a floating rate?
  3. What is a split loan?
  4. How do I choose?
  5. What happens when my fixed term ends?

What is a fixed rate?

Your interest rate stays the same for an agreed term, so repayments are predictable.

  • Ending a fixed rate early can mean a break fee.

What is a floating rate?

The rate can change at any time. You can usually make extra repayments or repay early without a break fee.

What is a split loan?

Part of the loan is fixed and part is floating, or the fixed part is split across different terms.

How do I choose?

Think about how much certainty you want, whether you plan to repay faster, and if you might sell soon.

  • We do not predict rates. We help you build a structure that suits your plans.

What happens when my fixed term ends?

Your lender will offer new rates. It is a good time to review your whole loan.

What is mortgage cashback?

A cash payment some lenders make when you take out or move a home loan with them.

What is a clawback?

If you leave the lender within a set period, you may have to repay some or all of the cashback.

Is the biggest cashback the best deal?

Not always. A higher rate or fees can cost more than the cashback over time.

Can I get cashback when refinancing?

Some lenders offer it to people moving their loan. Check break fees on your current loan first.

Can a broker help?

Yes. We can compare cashback offers alongside rates and fees, so you see the full cost.
