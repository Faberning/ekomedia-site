Why do I leak when I run, jump or lift?

It's usually stress incontinence. Pressure inside your body builds faster than your pelvic floor can respond, so a little urine escapes.

Running, skipping, double unders, box jumps and heavy lifts all spike that pressure. It isn't always about a weak pelvic floor: tightness, fatigue or poor coordination can cause it too. That's why doing more and more pelvic floor exercises isn't always the answer.

It's common, and it isn't only a postpartum thing. It can happen at any stage of life.

How should leakproof underwear fit for exercise?

Snug, not tight. Too tight and they can shift and pull the gusset out of place. Too loose and they can gap. Either way, that's when leaks get through.

  • Measure your high hip, at the top of your hip bones. That decides where the gusset sits.
  • Between sizes? Size up.
  • The waistband can feel firm at first and relaxes with wear. Give it a gentle stretch before your first session.
  • Check the gusset sits where it should, the leg openings feel secure, and they stay put when you squat and jump.

Do I need a liner too, and how do I wash them?

No liner or pad needed. Wear them on their own. Wash them before the first wear to activate the absorbency, then hand or machine wash under 30°C and hang them to dry.

Skip the dryer, the iron and fabric conditioner. Heat is hard on the bonded seams.

09 · When to get help

When should I see a pelvic floor physio?

Any time leaks are changing how you train. You don't need to wait until it's a big issue.

A pelvic floor physio can work out whether the cause is weakness, tightness or coordination, and build a plan around you. Treatment is often highly effective and doesn't always involve medication or surgery.

This page is general information, not personalised medical advice. Please see a qualified health professional for assessment and treatment.

  • You leak during workouts or everyday movement
  • You feel a sudden urgency to go
  • You avoid exercises like running or skipping
  • You wear pads to train
  • You feel heaviness, pressure or discomfort
  • You've noticed changes after pregnancy or menopause

Keep training. Work on it as you go.

Double Undies won't fix the cause, and a pelvic floor physio is the place to start for that. They're there so a leak doesn't decide which session you do.
